Solving problems alone is not the same as solving them in an interview. In a real interview you have to think
out loud, handle hints, manage time and stay calm while someone watches. Mock interviews are the best way to
practice that, and you can do them for free.

## What a good mock interview looks like

- **45 to 60 minutes**, like a real interview
- **One problem** (sometimes two), chosen by the interviewer, not you
- **Talk through everything**: clarifying questions, your approach, complexity, edge cases
- **Feedback at the end** on communication, problem solving and code quality, not just "did it pass"

## How to practice coding interviews for free

1. **Pair up with a peer.** Take turns as interviewer and candidate. Being the interviewer teaches you what
   good answers sound like.
2. **Use a shared editor or a whiteboard**, not your usual IDE with autocomplete.
3. **Time-box it.** Stop at the time limit even if you are not done, then review.
4. **Write down feedback** after each session and pick one thing to fix next time.
5. **Mix in daily problem practice** to build pattern recognition between mocks.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Jumping into code before clarifying the problem
- Going silent while thinking (narrate, even if it feels awkward)
- Ignoring edge cases and complexity until the interviewer asks
- Only practicing problems you already know how to solve
